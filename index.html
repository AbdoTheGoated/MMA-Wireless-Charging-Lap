WIRELESS CHARGING LAB

Charge without cables.

How does wireless charging actually work?

Wireless charging sends energy across a small gap using a changing magnetic field (not radio waves) instead of a cable. In this lab you will see it, play with it and measure it.

Start exploringJump to the lab

The idea

A charging pad and a phone each hide a coil of wire. Energy jumps from one coil to the other through a magnetic field.

The goal

Understand not only how it works, but why distance, alignment and heat change how well it charges.

Induction in 10 seconds

A changing magnetic field creates a voltage in a nearby coil, and that voltage drives a current. That is electromagnetic induction.

About the model (simulated estimates)

The pad's transmitter coil (12 turns, 2.4 cm radius) and the phone's receiver coil (14 turns, 1.8 cm radius) are modeled as circular coils. The mutual inductance M is computed numerically from their geometry with Neumann's formula for the position (x, y, z) and tilt (θ). Then k = M / √(L1·L2).

The pad drives a regulated 1.8 A (RMS) current at 127 kHz and the phone circuit is tuned to it. Induced voltage = ωM·I1, received power = I2²·R_load, and coil efficiency = received power / (received power + coil losses). It does not include the charger electronics, so it is higher than a real whole-system figure. Relative transfer compares the power with the best position the model allows (centered, 0.5 cm gap, flat).

The same coil model also drives Exp 02, Exp 03 and Factors, with about 85% added for the electronics (so the best case is near 79%, real Qi chargers measure roughly 60 to 80%). Limits: coils are simplified, no ferrite shield, no metal objects, and the charger keeps a fixed current, while a real one would raise its drive when the phone is misaligned. In this game there is no heat and no battery behavior. k, efficiency and battery charging are three different things. Coil values are for teaching, not measured device data. The field lines are the real field of the transmitter loop, drawn for learning, not moving electrons.
